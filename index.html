CASE 001CRIMINAL DEDUCTION

The Three Accusations

Exactly one person stole the diamond, and exactly one of the three statements is true. Who stole it?

ANSWER: BEN

If Ben stole the diamond, Ava's accusation is true, while Ben's and Cara's statements are false. That gives exactly one true statement. If Ava stole it, two statements would be true; if Cara stole it, none would be true. Ben is the only possible thief.

CASE 002LOCKED-ROOM MYSTERIES

The Silent Vault

Which employee access card opened the vault during the disappearance window?

ANSWER: DANIEL'S ACCESS CARD

The access log records Daniel's card as the only successful vault entry between 9:00 and 9:10 p.m. The other listed cards were used at different locations. This identifies the card used, but does not independently prove who physically carried it.

CASE 003HIDDEN EVIDENCE

The Dusty Rectangle

What does the dust pattern suggest, and what does it fail to prove?

ANSWER: AN OBJECT WAS RECENTLY REMOVED OR MOVED

The dust-free rectangle suggests that an object had been resting in that spot and was moved. Its shape may fit a large book, but the pattern alone cannot prove that the missing journal was there or identify who moved it.

CASE 004SECRET CODES & MESSAGES

The Message in the Mirror

Decode the Atbash message: GSV WLLI RH ZQZI.

ANSWER: THE DOOR IS AJAR

Atbash replaces each letter with its opposite in the alphabet: A with Z, B with Y, C with X, and so on. Applying it to the ciphertext gives “THE DOOR IS AJAR.”

CASE 005MASTER DETECTIVE CASES

The Four Envelopes

Exactly two envelope statements are true. Which envelope contains the key?

ANSWER: ENVELOPE C

If the key is in C, statements A and D are true, while B and C are false. That gives exactly two true statements. If the key is in A, B, or D, the number of true statements is not two. Therefore, the key is in C.
